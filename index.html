How Scholar Match works

The method, the data and the limits — so you can trust what you see.

Why this exists

UN Sustainable Development Goal 4 calls for inclusive, equitable, quality education for everyone. Target 4.3 asks for equal access to affordable university and technical education, and target 4.b asks the world to expand the scholarships available to students who need them. Billions in aid go unclaimed partly because students can't tell which awards they qualify for or which ones are worth the effort. Scholar Match answers both questions in one place.

Finding matches

Scholar Match uses a curated database of programs — major national scholarships plus the main state-funded grants and scholarships in all 50 states and D.C. — rather than scraping the web, because scraped listings are full of expired awards, paid “scholarship services” and outright scams. Each record stores the hard rules (education level, minimum GPA, residency, citizenship, income limits, required background or field) and what the judges weigh. A scholarship only appears when you pass every hard rule. Turn on Almost eligible to see awards where you miss exactly one soft requirement, like a GPA within 0.25 or a missing research project.

The search box at the top reads a plain-English description of you. With AI on, Claude turns it into profile settings; without AI, a built-in parser picks up GPA, grade level, state, interests and background from keywords.

Estimating your odds

Every estimate starts from the program's base rate: awards divided by applicants where those numbers are published, or a researched estimate where they aren't. That rate is then adjusted for how well your profile matches what the program weighs.

profile strength S = Σ (weight × your score) / Σ weight (0 to 1) your odds = base rate × e^(2.6 × (S − 0.55)) × fit bonuses range shown = odds × 0.6 to odds × 1.6 value for effort = award amount × your odds

A student who exactly matches a typical applicant gets roughly the base rate; a standout profile gets up to about four times it; a weak fit gets about a quarter. Government grants such as Pell, the Arizona Promise Program and the AmeriCorps education award are entitlements: if you qualify and apply on time, you get them, so they show as very likely. Random drawings show their raw odds and ignore your profile.

These are estimates, not promises. Essays, recommendations and interviews carry real weight that no model can see, so treat the number as a way to rank where your time goes.

State aid

Every state runs its own grants and scholarships, and for most students they are worth more than any single private award. Scholar Match includes each state's flagship programs: need-based grants (awarded through the FAFSA, often first come, first served), merit programs such as Georgia HOPE, Florida Bright Futures and Louisiana TOPS that pay automatically when you meet the cutoffs, and promise programs that make community college free. Several states also accept undocumented students through their own aid application; those programs are marked for DACA / undocumented status. State details come from College Ready Parent's state guides (checked against each agency in August 2026) and the agencies' own pages. For local awards in your city or county, use Find more with AI.

Deadlines

Most programs run on the same yearly calendar, so Scholar Match stores each program's usual dates and works out the next one from today. Programs marked Date checked Oct 2026 were confirmed against a live source in October 2026; the rest show their typical timing. High school seniors see awards whose senior-year deadline has already passed under Missed this cycle, and juniors see senior-year awards under Plan ahead so they can prepare a year early. Always confirm the date on the official site before you apply.

Privacy

Your profile and saved list live only in this browser. Nothing is sent anywhere until you press an AI button; then your profile summary goes to Claude on your own Claude account to answer that one request.

Staying safe

  • Never pay to apply for a scholarship or to “guarantee” an award.
  • Real programs don't ask for bank details or a Social Security number up front (the FAFSA is the exception, on studentaid.gov).
  • Apply through the official site linked on each card.
  • AI suggestions under Look beyond this list are unverified until you find them on the provider's own site.
